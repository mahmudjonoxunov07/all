Web Accessibility (A11y)

Designing for all users means making the web work across visual, motor, cognitive, and hearing needs.

Week 9 · Dr. Toshpulatov

A better experience for everyone

What is accessibility?

Accessibility (A11y) is the practice of designing and building experiences people can use, whatever their abilities or how they access the web.

01

Visual

Blindness, low vision, and color blindness

About 2.2 billion people globally experience vision impairment. Use text alternatives and sufficient contrast.
02

Motor

Tremors or limited arm mobility

Make every action possible with a keyboard.
03

Cognitive

Dyslexia, ADHD, or memory impairments

Use clear language and consistent layouts.
04

Hearing

Deafness or hard of hearing

Provide captions, transcripts, and visual cues.

1 in 6 people worldwide has a disability. Accessible sites can also improve SEO, load faster, and reach more users.

A practical framework

WCAG: four principles

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines organize accessibility around four goals: content should be perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust.

P

Perceivable

Users can see, hear, or otherwise perceive all content.

  • Alt text on images
  • Captions on video
  • Sufficient color contrast
O

Operable

Users can navigate and interact without a mouse.

  • Full keyboard access
  • No keyboard traps
  • Skip navigation links
U

Understandable

Content and interactions are clear and predictable.

  • Consistent navigation
  • Plain language
  • Helpful error messages
R

Robust

Content works with current and future assistive technology.

  • Valid semantic HTML
  • ARIA used correctly
  • Future-proof markup

Course target: WCAG 2.1 Level AA. Level A is the minimum, AA is the industry-standard target, and AAA is the highest level.

Quick quiz · 01

What is the minimum WCAG 2.1 AA contrast ratio for normal body text?

  1. 2:1
  2. 3:1
  3. 4.5:1
  4. 7:1
Reveal the answer

4.5:1. Large text (at least 18pt regular or 14pt bold) needs a minimum ratio of 3:1. Level AAA requires 7:1 for normal text. Try the WebAIM Contrast Checker or Chrome DevTools.

Meaning beats markup

Semantic HTML vs. “div soup”

Semantic elements tell assistive technology what each part of a page means. Use the element that matches the content instead of generic containers everywhere.

Div soup · no meaning

<div>Home</div>
<div>About</div>
<div>Article Title</div>
<div>Content here</div>
<div>Copyright 2026</div>

Browser output

Home
About
Article Title
Content here
Copyright 2026

For a screen reader: the text is available in order, but there are no navigation landmarks or heading structure.

Semantic HTML · clear landmarks

<header>Course site</header>
<nav aria-label="Main">
  <a href="#page-title">Home</a> · <a href="#semantic-heading">About</a>
</nav>
<main>
  <h1>Article Title</h1>
  <p>Content here</p>
</main>
<footer>Copyright 2026</footer>

Browser output

Course site
Home · About

Article Title

Content here

Copyright 2026

A screen reader can identify: banner, navigation, main, and footer landmarks, plus an H1 heading.

Quick quiz · 02

Which element is the most accessible way to mark up a site’s navigation links?

  1. <div>
  2. <nav>
  3. <section>
  4. <span>
Reveal the answer

<nav>. It creates a navigation landmark so assistive technology users can find and move between important link groups.
